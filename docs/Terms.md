HR and company structure

CTC (Cost to Company): the total yearly amount the company spends on an employee: salary plus employer PF and other benefits. It is not the same as take-home pay.
Grade G1–G5: seniority levels. They decide who approves your leave (see approval chain).
Employee master: the main data table of employees (grade, CTC, joining date, location, bank details, tax regime).
Tax regime: India has an old and a new income-tax system. The employee picks one. The new regime is generally the default if they don’t declare otherwise, according to this TDS guide. The brief says switching mid-year must be rejected.
Metro / non-metro: decides how much HRA you get (50% vs 40% of basic).

Leave types

CL (Casual Leave): short personal leave. It accrues 1 per month, caps at 12, and lapses at year-end.
SL (Sick Leave): 1 per month, caps at 12, and carries forward up to 30. More than 3 consecutive days needs a medical flag.
EL (Earned/Privilege Leave): 1.5 per month, credited quarterly in arrears (after the quarter ends, e.g. 4.5 days credited in April for Jan–Mar). It carries forward up to 45 and needs 7 days’ notice.
LOP (Loss of Pay): unpaid leave. Those days are deducted from salary.
Comp-off: a day off earned for working on a holiday or weekly-off. Here it expires in 60 days.
Maternity: a special long leave. It overlaps payroll runs, so it needs careful handling.
Accrual: the leave you “earn” over time (e.g. CL 1/month on the 1st).
Carry-forward / lapse: at year-end, some leave rolls into next year (up to the cap) and the rest vanishes.
Pro-rata: a proportional share. A joiner on or before the 15th gets that month’s accrual. On the 16th or later, they get none.
Sandwich rule: if weekly-offs or holidays fall between two leave days, they are also counted as leave. For example, leave on Friday and Monday can count as four days, per this explainer. It is company policy rather than a universal law. In your brief it applies to EL and LOP only, not CL/SL.
Approval chain: G1–G2 need the manager. G3–G4 need manager and HR. G5 needs HR only. Requests auto-approve after 5 working days, except LOP.
Auto-convert to LOP: if the balance is insufficient, the excess days become LOP with a flag.
Attendance

Each day is one of: present, absent, half-day (counts 0.5), holiday, weekly-off, or WFH (counts as present).

Payable days = calendar days − LOP − unauthorised absences. This is the number that pro-rates the salary.

Salary components

Basic: 40% of CTC.
HRA (House Rent Allowance): 50% of basic in metro cities, 40% elsewhere.
Special allowance: the balancing figure so that all components add up to CTC.
Gross: total earnings before deductions.
Net pay: what lands in the bank after deductions.
Employer costs: what the company pays on top, such as employer PF.
Payslip: the monthly statement showing earnings, deductions and net pay.
Bank advice: a file or list telling the bank whom to pay and how much.
Mid-month joiner/exit and salary revision effective any date: salary must be split proportionally when something changes mid-month.

Statutory deductions (government-mandated)

PF (Provident Fund): retirement savings. The employee pays 12% of basic and the employer matches. It is limited to a wage ceiling of ₹15,000 (so ₹1,800 each), and anything above is optional, as covered in this EPF explainer and this report on the 2026 EPF Scheme. That is what “opt-out of excess allowed” refers to.
Professional Tax (PT): a small state tax on salary. Karnataka collects ₹200 per month and ₹300 in February, so the year totals ₹2,500. See Karnataka PT details.
TDS (Tax Deducted at Source): the employer deducts income tax from salary every month and pays it to the government. The employer estimates the year’s tax and deducts it monthly (Section 192), per this Section 192 guide. Your brief’s formula is: (projected annual tax − TDS already deducted) ÷ remaining months, recomputed every month.
Form 16: the yearly certificate of salary paid and tax deducted, per this overview. Your “Form-16-style annual tax summary” is a simplified version.

Exit terms

Full and Final (F&F): the final settlement when someone leaves. It includes pay up to the last day, EL encashment (unused EL paid out at basic/30 per day), notice shortfall recovery (if they leave without serving the notice period, the company recovers gross/30 per short day), and asset deductions (laptop not returned and so on). Release is after 45 days.
Exit during notice with negative balance: the employee has taken more leave than they had.
